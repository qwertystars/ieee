# Motor start-threshold variance, minimum throttle / motor idle, and multi-motor bench symptoms (SimonK 30A + 2212 1000KV + ESP32, 3S)

Research method note: all web access was through the TinyFish CLI (`tinyfish search query` and `tinyfish fetch content get`). The `--highlights` option returned 403 (not enabled), so full pages were fetched and grepped. The SimonK firmware source (`tgy.asm`) was downloaded with `curl` from raw.githubusercontent.com (a file download, not page reading) so the startup logic could be read directly. Some RCGroups and StackExchange pages returned empty content and are cited only from search snippets, marked "(search snippet)".

Firmware scope: **[SimonK]** means specific to SimonK/tgy, **[BLHeli]** means BLHeli/BLHeli_S/Bluejay, **[generic]** means any sensorless ESC or flight controller.

## Q1. Why do motors start at different throttle values even after ESC calibration?

### Takeaway
A spread of 1–2% in start throttle between "identical" ESC/motor sets is normal and documented (ArduPilot). On SimonK there are three firmware-level causes: (a) the ESC runs on an internal RC oscillator that differs 5–10% between boards and drifts with temperature and supply voltage, so calibration is only exact at the temperature and voltage it was done at; (b) the sensorless start routine limits starting duty to the *commanded* throttle (capped at 1/6 to 1/4 of the power range), so near the threshold each motor's friction, cogging position and prop load decides whether it syncs; (c) calibration deliberately adds a 1/16 low-end margin. With props on, the start threshold is higher and less consistent.

### Cited Findings
- [generic] ArduPilot: "Most ESCs have a dead zone at the bottom of their range." When you test each motor for the percentage needed to spin, "If all ESCs are from the same manufacturer they will likely all have similar dead zones, but having one or two different by 1% ~ 2% is common. Pick the highest percentage of all the motors." — [ArduPilot: Setting Motor Range](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/set-motor-range.html)
- [generic] Oscar Liang / CapeFPV gives an example of per-motor spread after calibration: motor 1 starts at 1120, motor 2 at 1130, motor 3 at 1110 and motor 4 at 1140 µs, so min throttle is set to 1140. The author's own build needed 1155. — [Oscar Liang: Linear Throttle Response / Motor Top End Calibration](https://oscarliang.com/linear-throttle-response-motor-top-end-calibration/)
- [SimonK] Boards without external oscillators (typically those using `tgy.hex`) "must use the internal RC oscillator on the Atmega8, which may be off by 5% - 10% between each board, and will also drift by 10% or more over a 40 degree Celsius temperature range. This can cause throttle differences between boards if not calibrated, and issues arming the ESC, particularly in cold environments." — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)
- [SimonK] If calibrating ESCs one at a time, "try to maintain a close temperature and at least 6-7V input voltage (the ATmega8 oscillator speeds up as temperature and Vcc decrease)." — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)
- [SimonK] Calibration margins: "While calibrating, margins of 1/16th on the low end and 1/32nd on the high end are used to try to avoid problems with arming and reaching full throttle during temperature extremes." Uncalibrated defaults are stop at 1060 µs and full at 1860 µs. — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)
- [SimonK] Startup source code (`tgy.asm`, master): `PWR_MIN_START = POWER_RANGE/6` ("Power limit while starting (to start)"), `PWR_MAX_START = POWER_RANGE/4` ("if still not running"), `PWR_COOL_START = POWER_RANGE/24` (reduces heating while stuck), `ENOUGH_GOODIES = 12` ("This many start cycles without timeout will transition to running mode"), `TIMEOUT_START = 48000` (zero-crossing timeout per commutation while starting), and `MIN_DUTY = 56*CPU_MHZ/16` ("Minimum PWM on-time (too low and FETs won't turn on, hard starting)"). — [sim-/tgy tgy.asm](https://github.com/sim-/tgy/blob/master/tgy.asm)
- [SimonK] In `set_new_duty` the applied duty is the *minimum* of `rc_duty` (the commanded throttle), `timing_duty` and `sys_control`. At start, `sys_control` is loaded with `PWR_MIN_START` ("Start with limited power to reduce the chance that we align to a timing harmonic") and steps up towards `PWR_MAX_START`. So during startup the ESC never applies more than the commanded throttle. — [sim-/tgy tgy.asm](https://github.com/sim-/tgy/blob/master/tgy.asm)
- [generic] A sensorless ESC "has no information as to the position of the rotor". It pulses power, reads back-EMF, and repeats "until the ESC and motor are fully in sync", which is where hesitation happens. Extra load (binding) "will make it more difficult for the ESC and motor to sync up", and "The startup synchronization process takes a lot of power... Weaker Battery packs will struggle". — [RadioControlInfo: How to Improve Cogging / Startup Hesitation](https://www.radiocontrolinfo.com/how-to-improve-cogging-esc-motor-hesitation-on-startup/)
- [BLHeli/Bluejay] On BLHeli-family ESCs, start behaviour can be set at runtime ("Minimum Startup Power"; Oscar suggests 1100 for tiny whoops and warns that too high "could actually damage your motors if they are obstructed"). — [Oscar Liang: Fix Tiny Whoop Motors that Won't Spin](https://oscarliang.com/fix-tiny-whoop-motors-not-spin/). A Reddit answer says "It's normal to have some variance between motors, and a borderline startup power value can easily cause some of your motors to hang up" — [r/fpv (search snippet)](https://www.reddit.com/r/fpv/comments/1spx2zg/kayoumini_motor_problem/)
- [SimonK] SimonK has no runtime startup-power setting. The values above are compile-time `.equ` constants in `tgy.asm`, so changing them means rebuilding and reflashing. — [sim-/tgy tgy.asm](https://github.com/sim-/tgy/blob/master/tgy.asm)

### Inferences
- The user's figures (fails at 10%, starts reliably at 15% with prop on) fit the SimonK logic. At 10% command the startup duty is only about 10% of range, with the prop's drag and inertia on top. The start-power cap (16.7–25%) only *limits* power and never *adds* any, so the commanded throttle has to be high enough on its own.
- Calibration cancels most of the oscillator offset between boards at the temperature and voltage where it was done. Mechanical factors (bearing friction, rotor cogging position, prop balance and inertia) and the remaining oscillator drift can still give a 1–3% spread, which is normal.
- Measure each motor's start point *with props on* at the battery voltage you will use, and treat the highest one plus a margin as the floor.

### Gaps
- I found no published measurements of SimonK start-threshold spread for 2212 1000KV motors with a specific prop. The 1–2% ArduPilot figure and Oscar's 1110–1155 µs examples are generic.
- I did not confirm whether the user's particular "SimonK 30A" boards have an external resonator, which affects how much the oscillator varies. The README says boards using `tgy.hex` do not.

## Q2. How do flight controllers handle this (Betaflight min_throttle / motor_idle / dshot_idle_value, ArduPilot MOT_SPIN_ARM / MOT_SPIN_MIN / MOT_PWM_MIN)?

### Takeaway
Flight controllers never send a "spinning" command below a floor set *per build* by measuring where the slowest motor starts. ArduPilot sets MOT_SPIN_ARM to the highest per-motor start % + 2%, and MOT_SPIN_MIN to MOT_SPIN_ARM + 0.03 or more. Betaflight's PWM-era rule was to set min_throttle "just above reliable spin up of all motors", typically about 1060–1155 µs. Modern Betaflight uses a percentage idle (`motor_idle` / `dshot_idle_value`, default 5.5%) plus RPM-based dynamic idle. Zero throttle and disarmed are handled separately (min_command = 1000 µs, motors stopped).

### Cited Findings
- [ArduPilot] Procedure: with props removed, use Motor Test to find the % at which each motor spins, take the highest, then "set the MOT_SPIN_ARM parameter to (the percentage discovered above + 2%) / 100. I.e. if you found the deadzone of the ESCs was 7%, set MOT_SPIN_ARM to 0.09" and "set the MOT_SPIN_MIN parameter to at least 0.03 higher than MOT_SPIN_ARM". Going higher is "acceptable if we want to account for voltage drop of the battery", but too high reduces low-end control. "Please complete the ESC calibration before setting the motor range." — [ArduPilot: Setting Motor Range](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/set-motor-range.html)
- [ArduPilot] MOT_SPIN_ARM is the speed while armed but not flying. MOT_SPIN_MIN is the lower limit once flying, so that "we never output a value that causes the motors to stop spinning". MOT_SPIN_MAX defaults to 0.95. — [ArduPilot: Setting Motor Range](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/set-motor-range.html)
- [ArduPilot] Community practice: "Increase the value until all motors always start reliably. This is MOT_SPIN_ARM"; "for a lot of multirotors MOT_SPIN_MIN is easy: MOT_SPIN_MIN = MOT_SPIN_ARM + 0.03"; MOT_SPIN_MIN "needs to be high enough that the motors will reliably remain turning in the correct direction and not desync during descent". A developer adds that you want it "as low as possible while being 100% confident that the ESC won't desync or burn out if you quickly apply throttle". — [ArduPilot Discourse: How to do Motor Test to determine MOT_SPIN_ARM and MOT_SPIN_MIN](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/how-to-do-motor-test-to-determine-mot-spin-arm-and-mot-spin-min/78283)
- [ArduPilot] MOT_PWM_MIN / MOT_PWM_MAX set the PWM range sent to motors and should match the ESC's calibrated range. ESCs that cannot be calibrated need MOT_PWM_MIN = 1000 and MOT_PWM_MAX = 2000. — [ArduPilot: PWM, OneShot and OneShot125 ESCs](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/common-brushless-escs.html); [ArduPilot: ESC Calibration](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/esc-calibration.html)
- [Betaflight] `min_throttle`: "Typically set to just above reliable spin up of all motors. Sometimes this is set slightly higher for prop stall prevention during advanced maneuvers". `min_command`: what is sent when disarmed (or below min_check with motor stop), "must be set well below motors spinning for safety". — [Betaflight docs: Controls](https://betaflight.com/docs/wiki/guides/current/Controls)
- [Betaflight] The current CLI reference lists `motor_idle` with default 550 (5.5%): "Idle throttle value sent to ESCs when armed (units depend on protocol). Used as the minimum non-zero motor command". `min_command` defaults to 1000 ("PWM value sent to ESCs when disarmed or at zero throttle (for PWM-based protocols)") and `max_throttle` to 2000. — [Betaflight docs: CLI](https://betaflight.com/docs/wiki/guides/current/Cli)
- [Betaflight] Dynamic Idle: `dshot_idle_value` "defaults to 5.5%. When throttle stick is at zero, the motors always get 5.5% motor drive"; the floor exists because otherwise props "may either turn so slowly that they can't start quickly when needed, or stop spinning completely... (motor desync)". In 4.2 dshot_idle_value is the minimum throttle; in 4.3 with dynamic idle it is ignored in favour of an RPM floor (`dyn_idle_min_rpm`, which needs bidirectional DShot RPM telemetry). — [Betaflight docs: Dynamic Idle](https://betaflight.com/docs/wiki/guides/current/Dynamic-Idle)
- [Betaflight] Oscar Liang: raise the Motor Idle value for low-throttle wobble; "Default is 5.5% which might be a bit low for low power rigs." — [Oscar Liang: Low Throttle Instability](https://oscarliang.com/low-throttle-instability/)
- [generic] Typical analog values reported: min throttle 1150 set after calibration, and "The Minimum Throttle Value is what is required as input for all motors to start spinning freely" (not the same as min_command 1000) — [Oscar Liang](https://oscarliang.com/linear-throttle-response-motor-top-end-calibration/). "Raising min throttle to 1060 - 1070 pretty much cures that" — [FliteTest forum (search snippet)](https://forum.flitetest.com/index.php?threads/quadcopter-motors-not-spinning.57536/). SimonK README example for Naze32/baseflight with uncalibrated SimonK defaults: `set minthrottle=1064`, `set maxthrottle=1864` — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)

### Inferences
- The same logic applied to this ESP32 controller gives three output levels. (1) **Disarmed / stop**: 1000 µs. (2) **Armed idle / spin floor**: the highest measured per-motor start point (with props, about 15% here) plus 2–3%, so roughly 17–18%. (3) **In-flight minimum**: idle + 3% or more. Do not command values between stop and the floor. Jump straight from 1000 µs to the floor, since startup is the weakest moment.
- The ArduPilot procedure measures with props *off*. This bench runs with props *on*, so the start threshold will be higher, and on SimonK the commanded throttle is what supplies startup torque.

### Gaps
- The Betaflight CLI page fetched (current docs) lists `motor_idle` but no `min_throttle` entry. I could not confirm from the docs which Betaflight version removed or renamed `min_throttle` and `dshot_idle_value`. Treat that version mapping as unverified.

## Q3. Is ESC calibration standard when using a flight controller, and how is min throttle chosen relative to it?

### Takeaway
Yes for PWM/OneShot ESCs: calibrate first (endpoints 1000/2000 µs from the controller), then set the spin floor (min throttle / MOT_SPIN_*) from measured start points. Calibration is not needed for DShot/digital ESCs. SimonK specifically says calibration "should be used whenever PWM input mode is used". The alternative is to set the controller's min/max to the ESC's built-in defaults (1060/1860), but that is reliable only on boards with an external resonator.

### Cited Findings
- [ArduPilot] "Most ESCs need to be calibrated so that they know the minimum and maximum pwm values that the flight controller will send." "If you are using a digital esc protocol like DShot or are using CAN ESCs then you also don't need to calibrate them." — [ArduPilot: ESC Calibration](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/esc-calibration.html)
- [ArduPilot] Order of operations: complete ESC calibration before setting motor range (MOT_SPIN_*). — [ArduPilot: Setting Motor Range](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/set-motor-range.html)
- [SimonK] "the input throttle range may be calibrated. This should be used whenever PWM input mode is used, including where external resonators are present". Alternatively "set the min/max motor output to exactly match the ESC defaults... ESCs with external resonators can use this method to avoid calibration." — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)
- [SimonK] Calibration procedure: full-throttle pulse at power-up (must be ≥ PROGRAM_RC_PULS, default 1460 µs), wait for one beep, go to minimum and wait for two beeps; then it saves to EEPROM and arms. "There is currently no way to reset (remove) the calibration other than by clearing the EEPROM (or reflashing without EESAVE set)." — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)
- [SimonK] Since 2012-03-15, "throttle calibration is disabled when a brown-out reset is detected". The author once accidentally calibrated an ESC after a NiCd pack browned out all ESCs. — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)
- [generic, Betaflight/Cleanflight] Set min_throttle 1000 and max 2000 *before* calibrating, calibrate, then set min_throttle to the lowest value where all four spin smoothly. "Do not calibrate your ESC's again after this" once the endpoints have been adjusted. — [Oscar Liang](https://oscarliang.com/linear-throttle-response-motor-top-end-calibration/)
- [generic] Warning: switching protocols without re-calibrating "can lead to uncommanded motor output". — [ArduPilot: PWM, OneShot and OneShot125 ESCs](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/common-brushless-escs.html)

### Inferences
- The user's `cal` command (calibrating all four at once to 1000/2000 µs) is standard practice. Calibration does *not* equalise start thresholds, so a measured spin floor is still needed.
- Calibrate all ESCs at once, at room temperature, on the real 3S battery, so the oscillators are at similar temperature and voltage. Re-calibrate if the PWM frequency or protocol changes.

### Gaps
- There is no source on whether calibrating at 50 Hz and then running at 400 Hz changes SimonK's measured pulse width. SimonK measures pulse length, not period, so it should not, but this is not confirmed.

## Q4. Multi-motor bench symptoms: one motor slows then surges at about 40%; one motor stops and keeps beeping with all four at 35% (each fine alone)

### Takeaway
The "stopped and kept beeping" symptom fits SimonK's **start-failed** state almost exactly. If a motor cannot sync during (re)start, the firmware cuts power and beeps every ~0.5 s *until throttle returns to zero*, then disarms and needs the arming pulse again. Two other things fit too: a **brown-out/reset** (rising-pitch reboot beeps, then the ESC will not arm at non-zero throttle) and an **RC-signal loss** (f3 f2 beeps). The beep pattern tells them apart. "Slows then surges" is typical of a **desync and re-sync** or a **brief ESC reset**, often caused by a poor phase or power connection, which matters given this bench's history of a snapped phase wire. Because each motor is fine alone but fails when all run together, suspect shared power wiring/connectors and signal ground/noise rather than the battery: a 3S 5200 mAh 40C pack is rated at about 208 A.

### Cited Findings
- [SimonK] Start-failed code path (`tgy.asm`): "If we were unable to start for a long time, just sit and beep unless input goes back to no power." It runs `switch_power_off`, then repeatedly waits about 480 ms, plays `beep_f4` ("Start failed" beacon), checks the throttle, and loops while the throttle is non-zero. Only when the throttle returns to zero does it go to `control_disarm`. — [sim-/tgy tgy.asm](https://github.com/sim-/tgy/blob/master/tgy.asm)
- [SimonK] While running, if commutation timing collapses (sys_control reaches zero) the code jumps to `restart_run`, which goes back into the start routine "from running". If the last commutation timed out with power off, it returns to `restart_control`. — [sim-/tgy tgy.asm](https://github.com/sim-/tgy/blob/master/tgy.asm)
- [SimonK] Beep codes: "f1 f2 f3: Regular startup"; "f3 f1: Voltage brown-out bit was set (MCU voltage dropped below 2.7V/4.0V)"; "looping f1 f1 f3 f3: Watchdog reset"; "fast repeating beeps of f4 while idle: Noise on PWM input detected"; "f4 f4 f4 (a long f4 beep) indicates that the ESC is armed"; "If no input command is received for about 1 second, f3 f2 is beeped and the ESC returns to disarmed state, waiting for a valid arming signal." — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)
- [SimonK] "if the ESC resets while the motor is still spinning (such as after a brief power cut), it is possible that the induced current will cause some transient errors that will clear once the motor stops". Brown-out makes "all output pins go high-impedance in hardware, and an automatic reset when the voltage comes back". — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)
- [SimonK] The README's recommended validation: with the prop locked down, "try a slow sweep from idle to full throttle. Finally, try rapid throttle changes from slow to fast. The ESC and motor should run smoothly, never lose timing, and the ESC should not reset." — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)
- [SimonK] "Immediate PWM input to PWM output for best possible multicopter response (but NOT where soft start or really any significant current limiting is needed!)". `SLOW_THROTTLE = 0` by default, so there is no throttle-jump limiting. — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy); [tgy.asm](https://github.com/sim-/tgy/blob/master/tgy.asm)
- [generic] Desync "is the ESC losing the sync with the motors commutation... the ESC advancing to the next motor coil TOO soon (common if this happens from too low of motor rpm). Or could be not keeping up... (at or near full throttle)". "True ESC de-sync is typically separate from issues caused by poor wiring/soldering", but poor wiring "could cause the ESC to not have the Current it demands... Poor wiring's typical symptom is the when throttle is increased quickly". Sensorless ESCs read back-EMF zero crossing, and "If it misreads this then time if off and it could desync." — [RCGroups: What is ESC desync?](https://www.rcgroups.com/forums/showthread.php?2718564-What-is-ESC-desync)
- [generic] "The most likely case is that the ESC is resetting either because of current draw brownout or vibration induced brownout." — [RCGroups: What does desync look like? (search snippet)](https://www.rcgroups.com/forums/showthread.php?2920511-What-does-desync-look-like)
- [SimonK, field report] "SimonK firmware, Motor 4 stops each time desync occurs, quad can rearm but motor 4 does not spin up again until power cycle" — [Facebook FPV Racing Seattle (search snippet; low-quality source)](https://www.facebook.com/groups/FPVracingSeattle/posts/363706277329353/)
- [generic] "When a motor jitters, it usually indicates one of the three motor windings is not getting power. It could be a bad solder joint, a broken wire" — [FliteTest forum (search snippet)](https://forum.flitetest.com/index.php?threads/esc-beeping-rapidly-when-throttle-and-has-only-one-beep-at-startup.77761/). SimonK README: hearing only one or two of the three power-up beeps on a board that worked before means a FET has likely burned out; the motor may still run but "will sound bad, and power and efficiency will be reduced" — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)
- [SimonK] Signal integrity: with no pull-down, the input "may easily float high", and "touching the connector or connecting it later may drain the input and cause normal startup to occur, which may seem like the ESC is resetting while powered." — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)
- [generic, signal levels] ATmega8 (the SimonK MCU): VIH min = 0.6·VCC for I/O pins (3.0 V at VCC = 5 V). — [ATmega8 datasheet](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/en/DeviceDoc/Atmel-2486-8-bit-AVR-microcontroller-ATmega8_L_datasheet.pdf). ESP32 VOH min = 0.8·VDD, i.e. 2.64 V at 3.3 V. — [ESP32 datasheet (search snippet)](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/documentation/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf); [esp32.com forum (search snippet)](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?t=15891)
- [generic] Bidirectional MOSFET (I2C-style) level shifters "may not be able to drive the signal input on the ESC", so a push-pull buffer such as SN74ACT244/241 is suggested. In that thread (SimonK 30A + ESP32-S3) the real fault turned out to be missing arming (1000 µs held first). — [Arduino Forum: BLDC motor not responding to ESP32 PWM via level shifter](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/bldc-motor-not-responding-to-esp32-pwm-via-logic-level-shifter/1402897)

### Inferences
- **Fit of the "stopped and keeps beeping" symptom:** this matches SimonK start-failed (periodic f4 beep about every 0.5 s while throttle is non-zero, then disarm when throttle returns to 0) or a brown-out reset followed by the ESC refusing to arm because throttle is not at minimum. Either way the recovery is the same: command 1000 µs for 1–2 s so the ESC re-arms, then ramp up again. The firmware should do this automatically when a motor is commanded on but has stopped.
- **Which beep is it?** Telling the beeps apart is the key diagnostic. Rising f1 f2 f3 mid-run means the ESC rebooted (power). f3 f1 means brown-out. f3 f2 means signal lost for about 1 s. Slow repeating f4 while commanded on means start failed / could not sync. Fast repeating f4 while idle means noise on the signal line. Record audio or video during the four-motor test.
- **Why "alone fine, together not"** points to shared paths. Candidates: the power distribution and battery connector (voltage drop across a thin lead or a poor XT60/bullet joint, which grows with total current); a common signal ground (ESC black wires to ESP32 GND should all go to one point, not daisy-chained through long leads); and EMI coupling into the unshielded 3.3 V signal leads. The 3S 5200 mAh 40C pack itself (about 208 A rated) should not sag much for four 2212s at 35–40%. This is a calculation, not a measured figure.
- **3.3 V signal margin:** a 3.3 V output is above the ATmega8's 3.0 V VIH minimum (at 5 V VCC) by only about 0.3 V, and the ESP32's guaranteed VOH (2.64 V) is *below* it. In practice it usually works, but the margin is thin, so ground bounce from high motor current could cause misreads or "noise" detection. Remedies include a push-pull buffer such as 74AHCT/74ACT powered at 5 V, short twisted signal+ground pairs, and a solid common ground. This is an inference from datasheet thresholds. No source directly reports SimonK failing on 3.3 V.
- **"Slows then surges" at about 40%** is consistent with a desync and auto-restart (`restart_run`) or a brief ESC reset. The most likely causes on this bench are an intermittent phase connection (bullet connector or solder joint; this bench already had a snapped phase wire), or a supply or ground transient when all four accelerate together.
- **Suggested diagnostics from these sources:** (1) Swap the suspect motor onto a known-good ESC and channel, and vice versa, to see whether the fault follows the motor, the ESC or the GPIO. (2) Wiggle-test and reseat the bullet connectors, check the phase solder joints, and listen for all three power-up beeps. (3) Do slow sweeps and rapid steps per the SimonK README. (4) Log or monitor battery and ESC input voltage during the four-motor step. (5) Check the PWM signal on a scope at the ESC end with all four motors running. (6) Stagger the start (bring the motors up one at a time to idle), then raise all four together.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative source quantifying how often SimonK 30A ESCs misread 3.3 V signals. The evidence is datasheet thresholds plus forum anecdotes.
- I could not confirm the exact start-fail time-out on SimonK (it depends on commutation timing). The code shows `START_MOD_INC = 4` and `START_FAIL_INC = 16` with byte wraparound, but this was not converted to seconds.

## Q5. Does a higher PWM update rate (400 Hz, OneShot) help SimonK, and is it safe from an ESP32?

### Takeaway
SimonK was designed to accept any update rate ("minimum ~5 µs PWM low time") and supports OneShot125 aliasing. 400–490 Hz is the multirotor norm (ArduPilot's default RC_SPEED is 490 Hz). Higher rates help closed-loop flight control response. They do **not** lower the start threshold or prevent the power, connector or start-fail issues above. From an ESP32, the 1000–2000 µs pulse fits inside a 2.5 ms (400 Hz) period, so it is electrically fine. Re-calibrate after changing the protocol, and use 50 Hz only for anything driving a normal servo.

### Cited Findings
- [SimonK] Features: "Accepts any PWM update rate (minimum ~5microseconds PWM low time)"; "ICP-based pulse time recording (on supported hardware) for zero PWM input control jitter". — [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy)
- [SimonK] `RCP_ALIAS_SHIFT = 3 ; Enable 1/8th PWM input alias ("oneshot125")`. — [sim-/tgy tgy.asm](https://github.com/sim-/tgy/blob/master/tgy.asm)
- [SimonK] `RCP_TOT = 2 ; Number of 65536us periods before considering rc pulse lost`. The ESC disarms after about 2 s with no signal and plays f3 f2 (code comment "Disarm after ~2 seconds of no signal"; README says "about 1 second"). — [tgy.asm](https://github.com/sim-/tgy/blob/master/tgy.asm); [SimonK tgy README](https://github.com/sim-/tgy). These two sources differ slightly on the timing.
- [generic] ArduPilot: PWM frame rates "usually between 50Hz to 490Hz. The faster frame rates allow quicker control reactions"; RC_SPEED default 490 Hz; "be sure of the capabilities of your ESC before selecting a higher frame rate to avoid damage". OneShot125 divides pulse widths by 8. — [ArduPilot: PWM, OneShot and OneShot125 ESCs](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/common-brushless-escs.html)
- [generic/history] "Standard Plane ESC's update at a rate of 50hz... SimonK increased that to 400hz"; "50hz update rate isn't going to work well on a mini quad. Even on a bigger 450 sized quad you'll see MUCH better performance from a higher update rate." It also notes that early SimonK versions had desync issues with high-KV motors, later fixed, and that BLHeli gained a reputation for fewer desyncs. — [FliteTest forum: ESCs for drones](https://forum.flitetest.com/index.php?threads/esc%E2%80%99s-for-drones.37226/)
- [generic] OneShot125 uses 125–250 µs pulses. — [Oscar Liang: OneShot125 (search snippet)](https://oscarliang.com/oneshot125-esc-quadcopter-fpv/). There is an Arduino library for driving SimonK ESCs at 400 Hz — [superjax/ESC (search snippet)](https://github.com/superjax/ESC)

### Inferences
- For bench thrust and start testing, 50 Hz is fine. 400 Hz matters once a stabilisation loop runs on the ESP32. It shortens worst-case command latency from 20 ms to 2.5 ms and lets the ESC notice a signal loss sooner relative to the frame.
- Safety on the ESP32 side: keep the high time at most 2000 µs at 400 Hz (period 2500 µs, leaving ≥ 500 µs low time, well above SimonK's ~5 µs minimum). Make sure the GPIO idles low at boot (a pin that floats high on SimonK can trigger calibration or bootloader entry, per the README), and re-run calibration at the new rate. OneShot125 from the ESP32 LEDC is possible but not needed for this build.
- A higher update rate will not fix start-failed, brown-out or connector faults.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch Espressif LEDC documentation confirming timer resolution at 400 Hz. The claim that the ESP32 can produce a 400 Hz, 1–2 ms pulse with sub-µs resolution is from general knowledge and should be checked against the LEDC docs for Arduino core 2.0.17.
- I found no source comparing SimonK start reliability at 50 Hz versus 400 Hz.
